\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This document defines the Wind Farm Layout Optimization (WFLO) problem that you
must solve. It gives the decision variables, the topographical and spacing
constraints, the simplified \emph{Jensen} wake model, the turbine power curve,
and the objective function---the expected farm power. It is the \emph{theory};
the technical instructions for generating instances and evaluating layouts are
in the companion guide \texttt{wind\_farm\_layout\_optimization/README.md}.
The simulator, the instance generator and an interactive GUI are provided; the
\emph{metaheuristic is your task}. A random-search example and a
simulated-annealing starter kit are included only to show the interface and to
give you a template to improve.
\end{abstract}

\section{Assignment brief}
%==============================================================================

You are given a piece of land (a \emph{terrain polygon}), a set of forbidden
areas (\emph{exclusion zones}), a number of turbines $N$, and a \emph{wind
rose} describing how often the wind blows from each direction and how fast. You
must decide \textbf{where to place the $N$ turbines} so as to maximise the
farm's \textbf{expected electrical power}.

The obstacle is the \emph{wake effect}: a turbine extracts energy from the flow
and leaves a slower wake behind it, so a turbine standing downwind of another
produces less. Spreading turbines out reduces interference but is limited by the
available terrain and by a minimum-spacing rule.

You must implement \textbf{one trajectory-based metaheuristic} (simulated
annealing, tabu search, iterated local search, \dots) that calls the provided
simulator. You are expected to design the encoding, the neighbourhood and the
acceptance rule yourselves. \textcolor{gray}{\emph{No finished algorithm is
supplied: the random-search example and the simulated-annealing starter kit of
Section~\ref{sec:example} are a starting point you are expected to improve.}}

\section{The model}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Decision variables}

A layout is a set of $N$ turbine positions in the plane,
\begin{equation}
  X = \{\, x_{1}, x_{2}, \dots, x_{N} \,\},
  \qquad x_{j} = \bigl(x_{j}^{(1)}, x_{j}^{(2)}\bigr) \in \R^{2},
\end{equation}
to be chosen within the terrain. In code, a layout is simply a list of $N$
pairs ``\texttt{[[x, y], ...]}''. The search space is continuous; the
constraints below make its feasible set non-convex.

\subsection{Topographical and engineering constraints}

Let $\Omega \subset \R^{2}$ be the terrain polygon. The feasible set is the
intersection of the following:

\begin{description}
  \item[(C1) Inside the terrain.] Every turbine lies in $\Omega$ (in code, a
  point-in-polygon test):
  \begin{equation}
    x_{j} \in \Omega, \qquad j = 1,\dots,N.
  \end{equation}

  \item[(C2) Outside the exclusion zones.] Forbidden polygons
  $\{Z_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}$ (buildings, protected areas, \dots) must be avoided:
  \begin{equation}
    x_{j} \notin \bigcup_{m=1}^{M} Z_{m}, \qquad j = 1,\dots,N.
  \end{equation}

  \item[(C3) Minimum spacing.] Turbines keep a centre-to-centre distance of at
  least $\gamma$ rotor diameters $D$,
  \begin{equation}
    \norm{x_{i}-x_{j}} \ \ge \ s_{\min} = \gamma\,D,
    \qquad 1 \le i < j \le N,
  \end{equation}
  with $\gamma$ given in the instance (typically $3$--$4$).
  Constraint (C3) is what removes the ``stack them all upwind'' trivial
  solution and makes $\Omega$ effectively non-convex.
\end{description}

\subsection{The wind rose}

The climate is summarised by a finite set of $S$ scenarios
$\{(\theta_{s}, U_{s}, p_{s})\}_{s=1}^{S}$, where $\theta_{s}$ is the bearing
the wind blows \emph{towards}, $U_{s}$ its free-stream speed, and
$p_{s}\in[0,1]$ its relative frequency ($\sum_{s} p_{s} = 1$). The wind travel
unit vector is $n_{s} = (\cos\theta_{s}, \sin\theta_{s})$.

\subsection{Simplified Jensen wake model}

For two turbines $i$ and $j$ define the along-wind and cross-wind separations
\begin{equation}
  x_{ij} = (x_{j}-x_{i})\cdot n_{s},
  \qquad
  y_{ij} = \bigl\lvert (x_{j}-x_{i})\cdot n_{s}^{\perp} \bigr\rvert .
\end{equation}
The Jensen model \cite{jensen1983} assumes a linear, top-hat wake whose radius
grows with downstream distance,
\begin{equation}
  R_{w}(x) = \frac{D}{2} + k\,x ,
  \label{eq:wake-radius}
\end{equation}
where $k$ is the wake-decay constant. Turbine $j$ suffers a fractional velocity
deficit from turbine $i$ when it lies in that wake ($x_{ij}>0$ and
$y_{ij}\le R_{w}(x_{ij})$):
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{ij}
  = \bigl(1-\sqrt{1-C_{T}}\bigr)
    \left(\frac{D}{D + 2k\,x_{ij}}\right)^{2},
  \qquad
  \delta_{ij}=0 \ \text{otherwise},
  \label{eq:deficit}
\end{equation}
with $C_{T}$ the thrust coefficient. The factor $1-\sqrt{1-C_{T}}$ is twice the
axial induction factor. Superposing the contributions of all upwind turbines in
root-sum-square form, the speed seen by turbine $j$ under scenario $s$ is
\begin{equation}
  u_{j}^{(s)} = U_{s}\left( 1-\sqrt{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{j}} \delta_{ij}^{2}} \right)_{+},
  \qquad
  \mathcal{I}_{j} = \{\, i : x_{ij}>0,\ y_{ij}\le R_{w}(x_{ij}) \,\},
  \label{eq:speed}
\end{equation}
where $(\cdot)_{+}=\max(\cdot,0)$.

\subsection{Turbine power and objective}

A turbine converts wind speed into power through the cubic power curve (with
cut-in $u_{\text{in}}$, rated speed $u_{r}$, cut-out $u_{\text{out}}$ and rated
power $P_{r}$):
\begin{equation}
  P(u) =
  \begin{cases}
    0, & u < u_{\text{in}} \ \text{or} \ u \ge u_{\text{out}},\\[1mm]
    P_{r}\Bigl(\dfrac{u-u_{\text{in}}}{u_{r}-u_{\text{in}}}\Bigr)^{3},
      & u_{\text{in}} \le u < u_{r},\\[2mm]
    P_{r}, & u_{r} \le u < u_{\text{out}}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
The farm power under scenario $s$ is the sum over turbines,
$P_{\text{total}}^{(s)}(X) = \sum_{j=1}^{N} P\bigl(u_{j}^{(s)}\bigr)$, and the
\textbf{objective} (to be maximised) is the expected power over the rose:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \bar{P}(X)
  = \sum_{s=1}^{S} p_{s}\, P_{\text{total}}^{(s)}(X)
  = \sum_{s=1}^{S} p_{s} \sum_{j=1}^{N}
      P\!\left( u_{j}^{(s)}(X) \right). \;}
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
The simulator reports $\bar{P}$ together with the \emph{actual efficiency},
defined as the ratio of the expected power to the installed capacity,
\begin{equation}
  \eta(X) = \frac{\bar{P}(X)}{N\,P_{r}} \in [0,1],
\end{equation}
and the annual energy production $\text{AEP}(X) = 8760\,\bar{P}(X)$ (in the
instance's power units).

\subsection{Why the problem is hard}

The objective \eqref{eq:objective} is non-separable (a turbine's output depends
on all turbines upwind of it), non-smooth (the wake indicator
$y_{ij}\le R_{w}(x_{ij})$ and the kinks of \eqref{eq:power} are
non-differentiable), and multimodal (each of the $\binom{N}{2}$ pairwise wake
interactions contributes local optima). Together with the non-convex feasible
set of Section~2.2, this defeats gradient-based solvers and calls for
\emph{trajectory-based metaheuristics}.

\section{Starting points: random search and simulated annealing}
\label{sec:example}
%==============================================================================

Two starting points are supplied. The \emph{random search} below is the weakest
possible algorithm --- use it to learn the interface. The
\emph{simulated-annealing starter kit} that follows is a complete trajectory
method that you are expected to tune and beat.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=py,caption={The provided random-search example.}]
def random_search(env, iterations, seed):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    best_layout, best_value = None, float("-inf")
    for _ in range(iterations):
        layout = env.random_layout(seed=int(rng.integers(1, 2**31)))
        value = env.evaluate(layout)["expected_power_mw"]
        if value > best_value:
            best_layout, best_value = layout, value
    return best_layout, best_value
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent
Everything you write should follow the same pattern: produce a candidate
\texttt{layout} and score it with
\texttt{env.evaluate(layout)["expected\_power\_mw"]}. The design of the move
(neighbourhood) and of the acceptance rule is entirely up to you.

\subsection*{Simulated-annealing starter kit}

\texttt{SimulatedAnnealingWFLOSolver} implements the four ingredients of any
trajectory method: (i) a \emph{valid random initial layout}; (ii) a
\emph{perturbation} that moves one randomly chosen turbine by a continuous
Gaussian step inside the terrain; (iii) a \emph{geometric cooling schedule}
$T \leftarrow \alpha T$; and (iv) the \emph{Metropolis} acceptance rule ---
accept improving moves, and accept a worsening move of size $\Delta<0$ with
probability $e^{\Delta/T}$. Every improvement point is flagged with a
\texttt{\# IMPROVE} comment: a better initial solution, a smarter neighbourhood,
repairing infeasible candidates, an adaptive schedule, and alternative
acceptance rules.
